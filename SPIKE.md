## The six stages, measured

**1. The declaring altitude runs on `rnx`.** The Ring meta-court passes the 28 pinned stzu
fixtures (8 accepts with structural expectations, 20 rejects with their refusal fragment)
on `rnxc` and on the oracle, and the two runtimes print the same bytes on every fixture.
The Luau meta-court passes the same 28. `machine.stzu` reads as 7 declarations, 5 forms, 0
expressions, 3 refusals on both, the meta-court's own count.

**2. The forge is A/B'd too.** The court and the grammar forged from `machine.stzu` are
byte-identical whether the forge runs on `rnxc` or on the oracle (1,724 and 2,395 bytes).

**3. The forged court against `harb`, 120 machine fixtures (26 accepts, 94 rejects).**

    from the PINNED declaration (machine.stzu, 79 lines, 6 kinds, 23 clauses)
      30  agree with harb (verdict and fragment, or verdict and the fields the court claims)
      38  refused by harb on a rule BEYOND what the declaration can say
      52  the DECLARATION LAGS THE COURT: a clause, kind or type harb knows and the
          .stzu does not declare (HF-31)
       0  refused by both with a different fragment
       0  DEFECTS of the forged court (a fixture harb accepts and the court refuses
          for a reason that is not the declaration's lag)

    rnxc and the oracle agree on all 120 fixtures, under both declarations

The 70 "beyond" are the measurement the charter asked for: the rules a court carries that
its declaration does not -- a profile's kernel, a board's consoles, a menu of words, a
default, the order of services, a capability lattice, a reference that must resolve. They
are the `WORLD`, `REFINE` and rule seats the charter's section 3 names as the growth of the
declaring altitude, and this is their size on a real language: **70 of 120 fixtures, 94 of
the court's 1,443 lines of Zig**. The accept expectations the declaration cannot claim say
the same from the other side: `board`, `console`, `libc` (defaults), `order` (AFTER),
`granted` (the lattice).

**4. The grammar.** 200 derivations sampled from the forged GBNF are all sentences to the
forged court's parser; 186 of them are then refused at the court's own stages (a required
seat absent 144, a clause given twice 42), which is what a context-free grammar cannot say.
The engine's decoder (`gbnf_machine.zig`, through `StzGrammarRefusal` and
`StzGrammarAccepts` on `rnxc` in the package) holds the grammar with no refusal and accepts
`qemu_hello.machine` under the pinned grammar; `makeen_box.machine` is accepted under the
brought-up-to-date grammar (measured by hand, `accepts: 1`) and not under the pinned one,
exactly as the court refuses it at `SLOTS`.

**5. One refinement through the gate**, `hello.RESTART` from `never` to `always` on a copy of
`qemu_hello.machine`, five stages in `stzRefinableCode.ring`'s order: structural, constraint,
derivation, governance, trust.

## What it found, each a numbered finding in `haro/FINDINGS.md`

- **HF-30** Ring's ordering operators are numeric only: `"a" < "b"` raises R41 on the oracle,
  and `rnxc` orders strings and answers 1. The port's first run on the oracle found it; a
  Ring++ divergence to decide, not a defect of the port.
- **HF-31** `machine.stzu` lags `machine.zig`: 13 clauses, the `PEER` kind and the fleet
  kinds; `makeen_box.machine` is not a sentence of its own language's declaration.
- **HF-32** stzu v0.1 has no union type; `ADDRESS` (`dhcp` or a string) and `EGRESS`
  (`none` or a list) cannot be declared as the court reads them.
- **HF-33** The forge's first version lost its output directory on the oracle because a
  helper assigned `cOut`, the name of the top level's variable: Ring's own rule, that a
  function's bare assignment writes the global (analysis 2.7), reproduced in the spike's
  own code and caught by the A/B. `rnxc`, under Rule A, ran it correctly.
- **HF-34** The charter's section 10 said 54 fixtures; the file has 120. The number was
  07-SYSTEM's of 2026-09-12 and had grown.
- **HF-35** Two implementations of C6 3.2 fold a string's continuation differently: the Luau
  meta-court strips the whitespace before the newline as well, `harb` keeps it. No fixture
  exercises the difference.

## The second rung -- Haro v0.2, the `WORLD` and `REFINE` seats -- 2026-10-01

beyond the v0.1 declaration were read one by one and sorted by what a declaration would have

meta-court grew in `haro_lib.ring`, the forge in `forge.ring`, the generic court in
`court_lib.ring`, and the condition language is its own file; nothing in `rnxc`, the VM or
a sibling repository changed.

## The sixteenth rung -- Haro v0.16, NARRATE tells a run and a question -- 2026-10-03

**On the author's "narrate a run".** v0.13 gave a language a voice that could say what a
SENTENCE is. It could not say what a RUN did, and a run is what stzn is for: the machine
speaking back is a narration of an execution, not of its source. `NARRATE` gains `TELLS`, a
template per event of a run (`run`, `result`, and any step kind that plays a construct of the
RUN) or of a question (`ask`, `matched`, `ordered`, `cut`, `answer`), and two verbs answer in the
language's own voice: `narrate-run <file> <algorithm> <record>` and `narrate-ask <file>
<question> <rows>`. A voice may now tell and say nothing -- TELLS alone is a voice.

**The narration is a projection of THE run, not a second one.** `narrate-run` calls the same
`RunAlgorithm` that `run` calls, once, with a flag raised; each step records what it did as it
does it and the record is told afterwards. Nothing is re-evaluated, so what is told cannot
differ from what was done. A step under another is two spaces deeper. The flag is off in every
other verb, which every earlier stage proves by passing byte for byte.

loop said nothing of what happened. (3) A `cut` is told only when the limit dropped rows --

gave the same question without the limit.

match too many, ordered backwards, a cut told over nothing dropped, and a narration that runs the
