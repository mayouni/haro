~~~ The fixtures of rung 22 (Haro v0.22): the READER of the Python-shaped face -- rung 7 of the code-graph memo. Python-shaped text in, a Ring++ program out.
~~~
~~~ Hand-composed BEFORE face_read.py existed (fixtures first, prose second). A fixture is face text (`--- py`, the banner line left out: the court
~~~ checks the banner on its own) and one verdict:
~~~   `--- ring`      the canonical Ring text of the graph the reader builds (print_ring, which parenthesizes by Ring's precedence)
~~~   `--- refused`   an id and words its refusal must hold. The reader's ids: syntax (not Python), construct (Python the face has no word for), word (a face word
~~~                   used as the face does not use it), order (a definition's place is semantic on Ring), not-canonical (a spelling the face does not print for
~~~                   that graph); and every collision of the printer (v21), because a text the printer would refuse to print is refused to read
~~~   `--- lenient`   with `refused not-canonical`: the Ring the reader builds when it is told not to insist on the face's own spelling (K4 counts how
~~~                   often a model needs this)
~~~
~~~ THE RULE. The reader accepts exactly the printer's image (v21): it builds the graph from the text, prints the graph back, and the two must be the same
~~~ Python up to layout, comments, quotes and parentheses. So a text is read only when it is the face's own spelling of one Ring meaning (CODEGRAPH 3.2: one
~~~ semantics, many spellings, and the reader never gives a spelling Python's meaning). What the printer cannot tell apart the reader fixes once, and says so:
~~~ `x += 1` is `x++`; `x = plus(x, y)` stays `x = x + y`; `self.x` is `This.x` in a method and the bare region name in `__region__`; a catch has a binding
~~~ only if the handler uses the name other than as `str(e)`; `return ""` is a bare return; the three kinds of comment are `#`.
