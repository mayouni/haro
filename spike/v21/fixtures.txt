~~~ The fixtures of rung 21 (Haro v0.21): a second face of the program graph, in Python's shape -- rung 6 of the code-graph memo, as a PRINTER.
~~~
~~~ Hand-composed BEFORE face_py.py existed (fixtures first, prose second). A fixture is a Ring program and either the Python-shaped text the face
~~~ prints for it (`--- py`, the banner line left out: the court checks the banner on its own) or the collision it is refused for (`--- refused`:
~~~ the collision's id, then words its refusal must hold). The face's one rule: a Python reader must never take the program for something else --
~~~ what reads the same is spelt plainly, what Ring means differently is spelt with an explicit word or refused by name (CODEGRAPH 3.2, 5).
~~~ Four spaces a level, Python's own; names in the spelling of their first occurrence, since Ring folds case (F-18).
~~~ Fixtures 47 to 54 were written on 2026-10-20, before rung 7's reader, because a reader needs the face to be INJECTIVE -- one Python text, one Ring
~~~ meaning -- and the printer was not: `new K(x)` and a call of a function K(x) printed alike, `@x` and `This.x` both printed `self.x`, and `o.@x` printed
~~~ `o._x`, which is also a name Ring allows.

--- py
